CubeSat

I am building a CubeSat with a team of students at Yale. I am responsible for the design and implementation of the communications and control systems. The mission is to demonstrate an extendable gravity gradient boom(GGB) for nadir pointing. The GGB is fully custom and developed by the team(PCB, mechanical, firmware). I have developed the full firmware and an automated test bench for validation. This includes a full simulation environment, capable of projecting the entire mission duration both with SITL(Software in the Loop) and HIL(Hardware in the Loop) testing. Power budget and mock sensor data generation are included. I have also developed the communications pipeline for the ground station to reeive telemetry and uplink commands.

The CubeSat will be deployed from the ISS in Fall of 2027 and we expect to establish first contact shortly after.

PCHELICHKA

PCHELICHKA is an autonomous drone-bee, built as a continuation of my research. I came up with an innovative end effector for pollination without damaging the plant, and designed and tuned the control strategy, while my partner provided the hardware.

The first video is the first fully autonomous flight(very dear to my heart) in my living room with no safety precautions, and a lot of trust in the control system. The second video is a more recent flight with the new hardware. I spent countless hours tuning the control system and optimizing the video transmission for precise operation.

The drone uses only its camera feed, received over a digital wireless link, and inertial measurement unit for navigation and stabilization. The drone hardware is thus affordable without any high performance chips for video processing, which is offloaded to a separate processing unit(or just my laptop usually). The motors are controlled with a cascading PID controller from position to velocity to acceleration to pitch, roll, and yaw rate commands, which are then sent to the drone through the FPV radio. The drone is entirely autonomous form lift-off to landing.

I also built a dashboard for management of the system. It allows for easy setup of the drone and an interface to the AI I trained. The computer vision model takes images from the camera feed and looks for signs of diseases on the plants to alert the user of threats to yield.

BDR

Bulldogs Racing(BDR) is Yale's Formula Student team, which I joined my freshman year. I quickly became the software lead and was responsible for all software on the car(and some hardware too). From late nights in the lab for hardware bring-up, to on-track problem resolution, I was involved in the full development cycle of the car or at least of its electrical and software systems.

My first task was to design and program a PCB to monitor all 84 battery cells. Due to the high currents involved, I had to resolve multiple issues(EMI, ground charge depletion, etc.) that prevented reliable measurement. Due to the stringent timelines, I had to get creative with software filters and hardware makeshift solutions, but ultiamtely the car ran at competition, securing 6th place.

I developed a complete telemetry system using the Mravka radios(the next project described below) that collected all car data and sent it to a dashboard for real-time monitoring. This proved essential to solve a number of issues that surfaced on track days, and I was able to diagnose and fix them in real-time.

Mravka(Ant)

I was contracted by a local chain of paintball arenas to create a device for a king-of-the-hill style game. I designed and fabricated a total of 5 "checkpoints".

An esp32 is the "brain" of each checkpoint and controls 2 high-power LEDs through Darilngton transistors. A UPS battery powers the entire system, and the esp32 through a step-down converter. The checkpoints are wirelessly set up before play and measure the duration that each team controls the checkpoint, producing a winner at the end. They are also able to sync with each other through a dedicated NRF24L01+ radio. The first prototype was assembled on a protoboard.

Subsequently, multiple hardware revisions, now manufactured as PCBs, were made with the help of my partner to improve range and reliability. Changes included switching out the transistors for dedicated IC switches, better power converters and much improved radio modules, namely the SX1281. The UI was also incrementally improved based on user feedback.

Some of the challenges(and also what taught my most) were dealing with wireless communication reliability and power management while keeping in sync an entire mesh network. I spent days diagnosing range issues and race conditions between the different stations. However, the most important lesson was learning how to communicate with a customer and understand their needs effectively.

FPV Drone Flight Controller

A flight controller project for an FPV drone, featuring a custom circuit schematic and PCB layout, developed from scratch by me and a partner. Supports all major firmwares like Betaflight. Has been flight tested successfully.

The main challenges of this project were part selection and routing. Most parts commonly used for similar projects were hard to source, but we wanted to utilize as much flight-proven hardware as possible to reduce development risk and ensure reliable flight performance. Component placement and routing were, in turn, difficult due to the size constraints of the board.

Sturshel

Sturshel is a long-range Y-4 drone I built with a partner, featuring a fully original design. The shell was generatively designed and optimized for weight and crash resistance. The carbon frame was fully designed and manufactured by us, and the flight controller is our very own you can see above.

We had one distinct design goal for this drone, namely to not have the propellers in frame of the camera. This was achieved by using a Y-4 configuration, which is a quadcopter with two motors on the front and two on the back, even though we had started out with a traditional quadcopter design(last image).

The main challenges of this project were balancing strength and weight of all components, but it was particularly educational as we went through the entire design and manufacturing process.

SHARO

SHARO is a robot dog I built again with a partner. I was mainly responsible for the software of the robot, which was built from scratch(pre-AI too). I came up with a novel way to define the motion of its limbs with Bézier curves.

I also integrated computer vision models for facial detection and emotion classification, which made it particularly engaging for kids at various events. To fit all the code on a Raspberry Pi 4, I optimized the code with multiprocessing and moved the inverse kinematics calculations to Rust.

This control panel(desktop and mobile app available) is used to preview the camera and sensors while controlling the movement of the robot.
